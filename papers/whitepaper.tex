\begin{abstract}
CA-PQ is a post-quantum cryptography framework that refuses the usual trade-offs.
Instead of choosing between battle-tested classical ECDH and larger post-quantum KEMs,
it combines both---plus a deterministic chaotic-stream layer---under a single transcript,
in a single dependency-free Rust tree where every primitive is hand-written and every
claim is tested. Two PQ profiles (ML-KEM-768 at NIST level~3, ML-KEM-512 at level~1)
feed two hybrid combiners alongside a hand-written X25519 leg. The result, on commodity
hardware: $\sim$17k PQ encapsulations per second, $\sim$2.7k hybrid pairs per second,
828--1\,148-byte ciphertexts, zero third-party code, and a red-team file documenting
exactly what was attacked, what broke, and what remains open.
\end{abstract}

\section{The problem}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------

Three forces collide in real-world cryptography migration:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Quantum horizon.} X25519/ECDH falls to a cryptographically relevant quantum
computer. Lattice KEMs (ML-KEM/FIPS~203) are the standardized answer, but migration takes
years and ``pure PQ'' deployments lose the classical track record.
\item \textbf{Hybrid demand.} The conservative answer---combine classical and PQ so the
session holds if \emph{either} holds---is now best practice in TLS experimentation. But
hybrids double implementation surface and dependency load.
\item \textbf{Supply-chain reality.} Each dependency is trusted code. PQC libraries pull in
big-integer crates, RNGs, vendor SIMD intrinsics, and build scripts. Auditing the union is
impractical for small teams.
\end{enumerate}

CA-PQ's answer: a hybrid framework with \textbf{zero dependencies}, \textbf{deterministic
APIs} (no RNG to subvert), and \textbf{tested honesty} (every security claim paired with
the test that would falsify it).

%-----------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Design principles}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2em,style=nextline]
\item[Hybrid by construction, not by glue.]
The combiner is a first-class primitive
($\mathit{ss} = \mathrm{Hash}(\mathit{ss}_x \Vert \mathit{ss}_{pq} \Vert \mathrm{transcript})$),
not two KEMs taped together. Per-leg failure semantics are specified and tested
(X fails closed, PQ implicitly rejects).
\item[Zero dependencies, verifiably.]
\texttt{Cargo.lock} contains only \texttt{chaos-*} crates. Hash, sponge, curve, lattice
math, and wiping are hand-written from public specifications.
\item[Determinism as a security feature.]
\texttt{keygen(seed)}, \texttt{encaps(pk, coins, nonce)}---all randomness is
caller-supplied. No RNG means no RNG failures, no hidden state, and byte-exact test
vectors for every operation.
\item[No floats in the key path.]
The chaotic engine runs in Q32.32 fixed point: cross-platform bit-identical, no FPU
timing or rounding variance.
\item[Performance without compromise of auditability.]
Every optimization (AVX2/NEON/SSE2 tiers, Barrett reductions, batch APIs) is gated
behind differential tests plus full-length KATs, and every ``clever'' change that
measured $\sim$0 was reverted on record.
\item[Honesty as architecture.]
The threat model lists non-claims with the same care as claims. The red-team file
records defects found, including the methodology traps that produced them.
\end{description}

\section{Architecture}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c}
\texttt{seed32} $\longrightarrow$ \fbox{HYBRID keygen / encaps / decaps} $\longrightarrow$ \texttt{(hct, ss32)} \\
\hspace{2em}$\swarrow$\hspace{6em}$\downarrow$\hspace{6em}$\searrow$ \\
\fbox{X25519 KEM + chaos tail} \hspace{1em} \fbox{ML-KEM 512/768} \hspace{1em} \fbox{transcript + combiner} \\
\hspace{1em}{\small ECDH (classical)} \hspace{3em} {\small lattice (PQ cover)} \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{X leg} (\texttt{chaos-kem}, sysid \texttt{PQ04}).
Hand-written X25519 ECDH ($5 \times 51$-bit limbs, Montgomery ladder, addition-chain
inverse) wrapped with a chaotic-stream tail:
$\mathrm{tail} = \mathrm{keystream}(\mathrm{expand}(\mathrm{XOF}(\mathrm{shared} \Vert
\mathrm{transcript})))$,
$\mathit{ss}_x = \mathrm{Hash}(\mathrm{tail} \Vert \mathrm{shared} \Vert \mathrm{tr})$.
Contributory zero-checks on both sides.

\paragraph{PQ leg} (\texttt{chaos-mlkem}).
ML-KEM-768 and ML-KEM-512 from one generic core---NTT with compile-time twiddle tables,
SHAKE-based sampling, CBD noise, Fujisaki--Okamoto transform with implicit rejection,
batched matrix sampling, batch encaps API.

\paragraph{Combiner} (\texttt{chaos-hybrid}, sysids \texttt{PQH2}/\texttt{PQH1}).
Subseed derivation, joint transcript $\mathrm{commit} \Vert \mathit{ct}_x \Vert
\mathit{ct}_{pq} \Vert \mathrm{nonce}$,
$\mathit{ss} = \mathrm{Hash}(\mathit{ss}_x \Vert \mathit{ss}_{pq} \Vert \mathrm{tr})$.
Secure if either leg holds.

\paragraph{DEM} (\texttt{chaos-aead}).
XOR stream + 16-byte keyed MAC, heapless API, constant-time verify.

\section{The chaotic layer: what it is and is not}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------

The engine integrates a damped 4D quadratic flow (Lorenz-28 regime:
$a=10$, $b=8/3$, $c=-1$, $d=28$) in Q32.32 fixed point with RK4 at $dt=0.001$,
discarding $N_{\mathrm{TRANSIENT}}=1000$ steps and sampling every 10th step through
a bit-folding extractor. Measured largest Lyapunov exponent $\approx 0.6$--$0.9$
(positive: chaotic, single---\emph{chaotic, not hyperchaotic}, stated honestly after
re-parameterization from a collapsed regime the tests caught).

What it \textbf{is}: a fast local stream expander and KDF with 256-bit seeds, strong
seed sensitivity (independent seeds: Hamming $0.500$ flat from 50 steps), and validated
whiteness (NIST-style 8/8, \texttt{ent} $7.99999$, 200~KB soak).

What it \textbf{is not}: asymmetry. Red-team gates prove the negative---raw drive is a
slow predictable signal (lag-1 $r \approx 0.97$ at sync sampling) and every observer
synchronizes to every receiver on the same drive. Drive is never published, never
trusted. Worst case (\S\ref{sec:security}) reduces to the asymmetric roots.

Known nuance: rare materials capture bit-exactly by step $\sim$2000+ (periodic-window
dynamics). Production tails end at step 1640, related materials are unreachable (XOF),
tails are unobservable (hashes only)---INFO-level, sensor-pinned, fully analyzed in
the Red-Team Report.

\section{Security analysis}
\label{sec:security}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Per-leg foundations.}
X25519: RFC~7748 shape pinned to independent big-int goldens (clamping, masking,
ladder constant, inversion chain, encoding). ML-KEM: FIPS-203 shape pinned to
full-length KAT agreement with an independent transcription (both profiles; no
interop claim). BLAKE3/SHAKE: official vectors + split-invariance + rate-boundary
oracles.

\paragraph{Combiner argument.}
The transcript binds commit, both ciphertexts, and nonce; $\mathit{ss}$ binds both
leg secrets plus transcript. Tampering X fails closed (\texttt{None}); tampering PQ
yields unrelated $\mathit{ss}$ (implicit rejection, deterministic); wrong keys fail;
zero peer points fail closed on every combiner (subgroup confinement: clamped scalars
$\times$ torsion $= 0$, gated both sides---tested). Cross-profile confusion is
impossible by type (distinct sizes) and separated by sysid + hash domains (verified
by full-stack KATs in both profiles).

\paragraph{KEM hygiene.}
FO transform with constant-time select; re-encryption is unconditional (timing smoke:
valid:tampered $\approx 1$:$0.93$---no early-exit oracle). KEM MAC is keyed, verified
before use; AEAD verifies before decrypting (constant-time compare).

\paragraph{Misuse model (documented, tested).}
Nonce reuse degrades to stream-XOR properties (standard, not resisted). Caller RNG
failures produce weak-but-functional keys (deterministic API contract). ML-KEM dk
arrays need \texttt{burn\_dk} (plain arrays can't self-wipe; in-tree flows do).

\paragraph{What is explicitly not claimed.}
No chaos asymmetry; no NP-hardness/hidden-subgroup claims; 256-bit seeds targeting
$\sim$128-bit classical security; not constant-time beyond branchless swaps and tag
comparison (bit-level timing accepted, no secret-dependent exits/lengths---verified);
no external audit (the ask of this paper's readers).

\section{Positioning}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------

CA-PQ does not compete with broad PQ libraries on breadth (one lattice family, two
profiles) or with audited TLS stacks on deployment readiness. Its position:

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Dimension & CA-PQ & Typical PQ library \\
\midrule
Dependencies & 0 (verified in lockfile) & OS-scale trees \\
PQ + classical & hybrid by construction & usually one or glued \\
Deterministic API & yes (caller RNG) & usually OS RNG inside \\
Float-free key path & yes (Q32.32) & rare \\
External audit & \textbf{no (the ask)} & some \\
KAT depth & full-length, multi-implementation & official vectors \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Within the tree, profiles trade size for speed honestly: 512 is $\sim$1.6$\times$
faster with $\sim$30\% smaller wires at NIST level~1; 768 holds level~3; hybrids add
$\sim$60/828--1\,148~B over the PQ leg for classical + stream robustness.

%-----------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Limitations and roadmap}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------

In order: \textbf{external audit} (the blocker for any production use),
transient-margin maintenance, NEON/SSE2 Keccak--NTT completion detail (SSE2 NTT
explicitly rejected with numbers; NEON done), Kani proofs, TestU01/DIEHARDER
campaign. Non-goals: new primitives, protocol design beyond KEM+DEM,
masking/countermeasure engineering (documented stance).
